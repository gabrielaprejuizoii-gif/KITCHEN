Teacher’s view

Objectives: name and group kitchen objects; say what they are for (“We use a … to …”); say where things are (in, on, under, next to, between). Time: 5 · 3 · 8 · 6 · 6 · 8 (photos) · 6 · 3 min.

StageExpected / notes
1 WelcomeAccept any real answer. Follow up: “What do you eat for breakfast? Who cooks?” Simplify to yes/no questions; extend with “Why?”.
2 Reviewchop · boil/heat · bake · stir · spoon · knife · serve. Retrieval only; do not re-teach.
3 ObjectsAsk “What is this?” before flipping each card; Laura repeats after 🔊. Pronunciation (American): refrigerator /rɪˈfrɪdʒəˌreɪtər/ (“fridge” is fine) · dishwasher /ˈdɪʃˌwɑʃər/ · microwave /ˈmaɪkrəˌweɪv/ · colander /ˈkɑləndər/ · whisk /wɪsk/ · drawer /drɔr/ · cupboard /ˈkʌbərd/ (p silent). Follow-ups: “Do you have a ...? Where is it?” Extend with because. Simplify: accept one-word answers, then model the full sentence.
4 PurposeExpected: blender → make smoothies; grater → grate cheese; can opener → open cans; colander → drain pasta. Common mistakes: “We use to make”, “for make”, missing a/an. Do each item orally first, then writing.
5 WhereScene: microwave next to coffee maker; plates in cupboard; trash can next to sink; sink between coffee maker and trash can; soap under sink. Mistakes: in/on, “in the left”. Add behind / in front of orally with real objects.
PhotosAsk “What is happening?” Accept single words first (cooking, vegetables), then expand: “A woman is cooking.” Common mistakes: “She cook”, missing is/-ing, “She is use”. Follow-ups: What is she cooking? Do you cook like this? Add your own photos with the file button.
6 Real life1: plates in the cupboard, milk in the fridge, ice cream in the freezer. 2: toaster, coffee maker, cup, plate. Task 4 is the main production: Laura builds sentence by sentence, then tells you her dish in about 1 minute. Ask: “What do you put in it? Who cooks it? Is it easy?”
7 ExitDo not correct harshly. Note two things to review next time.
